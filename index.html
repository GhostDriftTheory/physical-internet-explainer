VISUAL MAPPING Bitcoin × Physical Internet

From Money Without a State to Logistics Without a Lead Coordinator.

Central thesis: reducing dependence on one central coordinator is what lets the network scale.

Start here — why AI is needed, and what it is not

AI is not the star. Formal verification is the foundation; AI is the enabling technology that makes that foundation usable at scale.

1
Scaling means not routing every decision through one coordinator

To scale the Physical Internet, there should be no need for a single lead operator to handle every inter-company decision case by case.

2
Replace it with independent verification

Instead, each company checks a decision on its own, against a shared set of conditions — no central referee.

3
The foundation is formal verification — but it is hard for non-specialists to use

Formal verification provides a rigorous way to check decisions against explicitly defined conditions, even when companies have different rules. But translating changing business requirements into formal specifications requires specialist expertise and substantial ongoing effort.

4
So AI becomes a practical necessity — as the enabler, not the star

Every new participant adds its own conditions to organize, update, and keep consistent — which quickly outgrows what people, or fixed software, can maintain by hand. AI helps with that preparation, so formal verification can be used in real logistics. This is a practical, operational necessity — not something proven mathematically.

The usual framing: "AI optimizes delivery."
Here: AI makes verifiable cooperation operable at scale.

This is about one role of AI — making formal verification usable. It takes nothing away from the value of AI for optimizing logistics itself; that is simply a different job from the one shown here.

Why compare with Bitcoin? To show, intuitively, why shared rules and independent verification — not a central coordinator — can be a network's foundation. (The mechanisms differ; the limits of the analogy are set out below.)

Why compare them? — a shared shift in network structure (topological similarity)
Conventional: centralized (hub & spoke)
The center's capacity to process and coordinate becomes the ceiling on growth
Conventional national money Issuance and monetary policy under states and central banks

Reduce dependence

Designed to scale without routing every decision through one coordinator

The scaling chain: 1 · Reduce central dependence 2 · Autonomous participation on shared rules 3 · Each party verifies independently 4 · Reduces dependence on central coordination capacity
Why it can't be copied directly — the decisive difference in how responsibility is handled
Bitcoin-type (fully digital)
Verifies only transaction validity (the protocol does not allocate responsibility)
Because it moves digital data, the protocol does not handle liability for physical damage or delay.
AI × formal verification (connected to the physical world)
Checks agreed handover and responsibility conditions between companies
Because it involves real cargo and incidents, custody duties around each handover are made machine-readable and checked against agreed conditions and measured evidence.

I Mapping of social structure and network principle

Axis Bitcoin Physical Internet
Dependence reduced State / central bank (fiat currency) Case-by-case control & coordination by one lead operator
Conventional constraint The state's monetary system / centralized control The lead operator's capacity, interests, and coordination cost
Basis of trust Cryptography, shared rules, distributed consensus Formal verification, shared rules, measured evidence, agreement
How participation works Join without central permission Join on shared conditions, without case-by-case coordination by a lead operator
Legitimacy of decisions Each node independently verifies transactions Each company independently verifies adoption / operating conditions
Decentralization & responsibility Verifies transaction validity (legal responsibility out of scope) Also verifies the consistency of each party's responsibility boundary and performance conditions
Greatest significance A value-transfer base that doesn't presuppose state currency A logistics-cooperation base not capped by a single company's capacity

II Mapping of technical architecture (7 functional comparisons)

Functional layer Bitcoin AI × formal verification
1 · Shared rules Consensus rules Participation, adoption, and responsibility-sharing rules
2 · Preparing what gets checked Transaction creation by a wallet AI formalizes and maintains conditions, and prepares candidate plans for checking
3 · Independent verification Full nodes verify signatures & UTXOs Parties check decisions against shared conditions; formal methods support the verification of selected model properties.
4 · Consensus / state PoW, blockchain (prevents double-spend) Approval & reservation state, conflict resolution (prevents double-booking)
5 · Execution / finality Broadcast, block record, confirmation Delivery, handover, confirmation of custody transfer
6 · Result verification Verify block validity and confirmation state Verify with measured evidence: temperature, location, arrival, etc.
7 · Participant cooperation Each node applies the rules independently Each company keeps its own conditions and decision rights while cooperating
Limit 1
Mining ≠ AI: mining helps secure the transaction history; AI prepares and maintains what will be verified (conditions, and candidate plans).
Limit 2
Consensus ≠ formal verification: Proof tools such as Lean 4 check specified model properties; consensus mechanisms address agreement on shared state.
Limit 3
What formal verification guarantees: consistency with defined conditions (the truth of measured values and the final legal judgment are separate).

III Unbundling the lead operator's case-by-case coordination and reassigning it to technology

* Each company stays the responsible party; only the central coordination work is reassigned
1 · Learn each company's conditions AI-assisted condition formalization
2 · Coordinate delivery candidates AI-assisted candidate preparation
3 · Judge conformance Formal / independent verification
4 · Resolve double bookings Consensus & state management
5 · Check progress / completion Measured evidence & result verification
6 · Clarify liability on incidents Preconditions + verification history

Conclusion: Bitcoin demonstrated how transactions could be verified without a central authority. For the Physical Internet, we propose extending the principle of independent verification to cooperation between companies — with formal verification as the foundation and AI making it practical for logistics professionals to use.

Research this explains: Physical Internet — Verified Scalability (GitHub) — on common-infrastructure onboarding, route-independent decisions, and the structural difference between point-to-point links and a shared base. It does not, on its own, prove the scalability of a whole real logistics network.
